Process mining

IncidentOps: how work really flows

Process mining on a service-desk event log: where real cases leave the intended process, which paths take longest, and where escalations bounce back and forth.

Status

The demo runs on Streamlit’s free tier and can take about 30 seconds to wake.

The problem

A fictional IT service desk records every step of every ticket: creation, assignment, escalation, resolution, closure and customer feedback. Leadership still works from manual exports and averages, with no view of how cases actually move from start to finish.

The same type of incident can take very different paths, with different cycle times, escalations and satisfaction scores. Without looking at those paths, teams cannot tell healthy flow from costly patterns such as tickets bouncing between teams or cases closed too early. It is the same question I asked when I ran inbound operations at Amazon: nobody was working slowly; they were working in an order that never finished anything.

What I built

  • See the most common paths (variants) that cases take, compared with the intended process and weighted by frequency and cost.
  • Find the real bottlenecks: waiting time and working time are measured separately at every step.
  • Spot escalation ping-pong: cases passed back and forth between teams, with the number of owner changes for each case.
  • Monitor closure quality: reopen rates, rejected resolutions and cases closed without feedback, next to customer satisfaction.
  • Export follow-up lists for the weekly operations review: ping-pong cases, closure exceptions and candidates for problem management.

Tour the demo

  1. Start at Command Center for the summary and the main KPIs.
  2. Open Flow to see process variants and where cases wait longest.
  3. Check Handoffs for ping-pong patterns and what escalation churn costs.
  4. Review Quality for reopen rates and how they relate to customer satisfaction.
  5. Open Enablement for first contact resolution and the problem backlog.

How it works

The analysis is split into six views, each answering one operational question.

Six views

View 1

Command Center

Summary KPIs, service level compliance, volume trends and an overall status view.

View 2

Flow

Process variants, waiting time at each step, the spread of cycle times and routing bottlenecks.

View 3

Handoffs

Escalation counts, owner changes, ping-pong cases and what that churn costs.

View 4

Quality

Reopen and reject rates, early closures and how they relate to customer satisfaction.

View 5

Intake

Channels, issue types, volume trends and how clean the incoming data is.

View 6

Enablement

Level 1 resolution by issue type, training candidates and a problem management backlog.

Key measures

MeasureDefinition
Cycle timeTime from the first creation signal to the final close signal for a case
SLA metWhether the cycle time falls within the service level agreement target for the case priority
Reopen rateShare of cases reopened after an earlier closure
Reject rateShare of cases with a rejected resolution
Escalation countNumber of escalations or higher-tier assignments in a case
Resolver changesNumber of ownership changes across a case
Ping-pong casesCases moved back and forth, with high owner churn and escalation
Customer satisfactionThe case-level satisfaction result, where feedback exists
FCR proxySolved at level 1 with no escalation and no reopen, as a stand-in for first contact resolution

Why the logic lives in SQL

Business logic sits in SQL views, not in the app code. Each view is the contract for one set of numbers, so the dashboard can change without touching how a measure is defined. Loading is repeatable: a hash of each event row stops the same file from creating duplicates when it is loaded again.

What this shows

  • Looking past averages to the paths, waits and handoffs that drive them.
  • Operations thinking from the floor: finding where the order of the work breaks, not who is slow.
  • Turning event data into decisions for three audiences: the process owner, the operations manager and the quality lead.
  • Business logic in SQL views, so every number has one explicit definition.

Limits

IncidentOps is a personal project on a fictional event log. It is not connected to a live service desk and cannot change tickets.

The patterns it finds are evidence to investigate, not proof of cause.
