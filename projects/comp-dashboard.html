The Problem

Compensation teams at mid-market companies rely on manual exports from commercial survey vendors and static spreadsheets to benchmark roles. These snapshots age quickly, don't connect to internal salary data automatically, and give no systematic view of where pay gaps or equity issues exist.

This project simulates TechNova, a fictional 500-person US healthcare technology company operating across five metros, and builds the analytics infrastructure that answers: are we paying competitively, and where are our equity risks?

TechNova is a realistic fictional entity — all 800 employee records, salary figures, and job grade bands are synthetic. The BLS OEWS market data is real, pulled live from the public API.

What It Does

  • Pulls live BLS OEWS wage data via API — P25, P50, P75 percentiles for all roles across 5 US MSAs (Austin, New York, San Francisco, Washington DC, Denver), batched in groups of 50 series IDs per registered-key request
  • Loads internal salary and job grade data via validated CSV upload, mapping roles through a 38-role custom SOC crosswalk with match quality metadata
  • Displays internal vs. market side-by-side with percentile toggle and colour-coded gap KPIs — target: defensible market position in under 2 minutes (OBJ-01)
  • Surfaces market position flags — roles where TechNova's midpoint falls below BLS P25 (below market) or above BLS P75 (above market) — with severity distribution chart
  • Runs pay equity gap analysis by job family and level, flagging gender gaps exceeding 5% for Total Rewards review (framed as monitoring, not legal compliance determination)
  • Shows geographic pay differentials across all 5 cities for any selected role, with vs-average and vs-TechNova-midpoint comparisons in $ and %
  • Logs all pipeline runs with status, record counts, duration, and error detection — full audit trail on the Data Management page

What This Demonstrates

  • BABOK v3-aligned FRD with 18 functional requirements, MoSCoW prioritisation, stakeholder map, and traceability matrix covering all FR-01 to FR-18
  • API integration engineering: BLS OEWS REST API — 600+ series per run, batched requests, registered key, error handling, and rate-limit response documented in OpenAPI/Swagger spec (FR-17)
  • UML sequence diagram covering three distinct flows — BLS pipeline, CSV ingestion, and dashboard read path — with actors, error branches, and data store annotations (FR-18)
  • Data modelling: 8-table PostgreSQL schema with FK constraints, unique constraints, and controlled crosswalk artifact with change log (NFR-01c)
  • Technology decision transparency: FRD specifies Power BI; implementation uses Streamlit with full rationale documented (cost, accessibility, chart capability)
  • BA scoping discipline: explicit in-scope/out-of-scope decisions (no HRIS integration, no offer workflow, US-only in v1) each with documented rationale

Pipeline Architecture

Two write paths feed one read path. The BLS pipeline and CSV ingestion write to PostgreSQL independently; the Streamlit dashboard reads from both. All writes are logged to pipeline_run_log.

BA Process

Problem Framing

The starting point was a recurring problem observed in mid-market HR teams: compensation data becomes stale between annual survey vendor cycles, but internal salary data is updated continuously (new hires, promotions, merit increases). The gap between when market data was collected and when a pay decision is made creates systematic risk — particularly for fast-growing job families where market rates move quickly.

The BLS OEWS is published annually and is free — making it a viable substitute for commercial survey data for roles where precision matters less than directional accuracy.

Scope Decisions

Excluded FeatureRationaleFuture Phase
HRIS direct integrationAPI connectivity to Workday or BambooHR adds integration complexity without changing the BA demonstration. CSV ingestion is structurally equivalent for portfolio purposesPhase 2
Offer simulation workflowOffer decisions require approval workflows and candidate data — adding those would shift the scope from compensation analytics to HRIS functionalityPhase 2
International pay modellingNon-US MSA data requires different API endpoints, exchange rate handling, and different regulatory framing — separate scope itemPhase 2
Commercial survey integrationRequires data licencing (Radford, Mercer) — excluded for portfolio; BLS data is publicly available without licencingPhase 2

Technology Decision — Power BI to Streamlit

The original FRD specifies a Power BI dashboard as the v1 front-end. During the build phase, the decision was made to implement in Streamlit instead. This is documented explicitly — not as an oversight, but as a deliberate trade-off with recorded rationale: Power BI Service requires a paid Pro licence for external sharing; Streamlit Community Cloud provides free, permanent public deployment. All FR-11 through FR-16 dashboard requirements are fully implemented in Streamlit. In a real project context, this change would be captured in a formal change request.
