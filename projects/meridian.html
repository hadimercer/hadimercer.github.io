Workstream health

Meridian: status that is calculated, not declared

A workstream health tracker for people who manage work as part of their job, where status is calculated from milestones, budget and blockers instead of picked from a dropdown.

The problem

Project managers have Jira and Asana. But analysts, consultants, finance managers and HR leads who manage work alongside their day job do not see themselves as project managers, and they drop those tools within weeks. HR leads live this every week with projects like policy rollouts, system changes and hiring plans.

The fallback is a spreadsheet with a red, amber or green column, updated when there is time on a Friday afternoon. It never warns you when a workstream is drifting. It shows whatever was last typed. Meridian’s central rule is that health is calculated, not declared: you enter facts (milestones completed, money spent, open blockers) and Meridian works out what they mean.

What I built

  • Get a red, amber or green status for every workstream, calculated from three measures: schedule, budget and blockers.
  • Answer nine setup questions when you create a workstream. Fifteen rules adjust the scoring to fit, so a client project with a hard deadline is scored differently from an internal initiative.
  • Invite colleagues as Owner, Contributor or Viewer, and discuss milestones and blockers in comment threads.
  • Get a staleness flag when your own updates fall behind the cadence you set.
  • See portfolio analytics: a 30-day health trend, schedule against budget, milestone velocity and blocker age.

How it works

Status is never a field someone picks. It is recalculated from the source data on every relevant change, from three measures that feed one weighted score.

Schedule health

Formula

SV = milestone completion % − time elapsed %

GreenSV ≥ −10%On track
Amber−25% ≤ SV < −10%Lagging
RedSV < −25%At risk

Budget health

Formula

BV = (planned spend to date − actual spend) ÷ total budget

GreenBV ≥ −5%On budget
Amber−15% ≤ BV < −5%Over plan
RedBV < −15%Budget at risk

Blocker health

Open blockersScoreDefault status
None100Green

Composite score

Weighted average

Composite = (schedule × 0.40) + (budget × 0.35) + (blockers × 0.25)

Green70 and above
Amber40 to 69
RedBelow 40

How the setup answers change the rules

Setup answerEffect on scoring
Hard or contractual deadlineSchedule amber tightens from −10% to −5%, and red from −25% to −15%
Client-billable budgetBudget weight rises from 35% to 45%, with amber at −3% and red at −10%
Blocked by an external partyBlocker age thresholds are halved, so risk builds faster
High or critical risk ratingThe band between amber and red narrows by 5% to 10% across all three measures
Review or closing phaseAny unfinished milestone more than 2 days past due triggers amber

Access and privacy

Meridian runs on PostgreSQL through Supabase, with more than 15 tables. People sign in with Supabase Auth, and row-level security on every user-scoped table means no one can read another person’s workstream without an invitation. No credentials live in the source code.

What this shows

  • Scoring designed as a requirements problem: every setup rule and its effect was written in the requirements document before the scoring code.
  • Consistent rules for everyone: the same data produces the same status, whoever enters it.
  • Access control that keeps each person’s data private, with sign-in, roles and row-level security.
  • 35 written functional requirements, prioritized and traced back to the objectives.

Limits

Meridian is a personal project on synthetic data. It is not a production system and has not been used at an employer.

Scores reflect the data people enter. A workstream nobody updates is flagged as stale, not reported as healthy.
