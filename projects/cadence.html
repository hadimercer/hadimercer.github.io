Cadence: four HR processes, one audit trail

Four connected HR workflows (headcount reporting, performance check-ins, merit review and attrition risk) on one data model, with structured intake, automated routing and a full audit trail.

The HR problem

Four pieces of routine HR work eat time every cycle. The monthly headcount report is rebuilt by hand. Merit eligibility is checked rule by rule, one employee at a time. Performance conversations go undocumented and get pieced together from memory at review time. And attrition risk is only noticed after someone has already resigned.

None of these is hard on its own. The cost comes from running them in separate tools, owned by different people, with no shared data. The rating that should decide merit eligibility sits in one spreadsheet, and the pay data that should inform attrition risk sits in another. Cadence runs the four workflows as one system, so each one’s output feeds the next.

What I built

  • Upload a monthly headcount file. Cadence checks every column against the required format, reports errors row by row, saves the clean records and calculates compa-ratio, headcount change and new hire flags.
  • Record weekly one-on-ones and monthly check-ins. They roll up into a quarterly review draft that the manager edits, rates and sends to HR for approval.
  • Open a merit cycle and see, for every employee, which of six eligibility gates they pass or fail, with the budget pool updating as managers submit recommendations.
  • Work from a ranked attrition risk register built on seven factors, and log the retention action taken for each person flagged.
  • Change the attrition factor weights on screen, without a developer. Every change is written to an audit log.

Tour the demo

  1. Start on the Data Upload page and load a headcount file to fill the KPI dashboard.
  2. Open the Workforce KPI Dashboard for attrition rate, compa-ratio by grade and headcount against budget.
  3. Walk the performance workflow: Weekly 1:1s, then Monthly Check-ins, then Quarterly Reviews.
  4. Open Merit Cycle and Eligibility to see the six gates at work.
  5. Check the Attrition Risk Register for the ranked view and the score behind each factor.

How it works

The workflows were built in the order the data depends on them: headcount first, then performance, then compensation review, then attrition risk. Attrition scoring means nothing until headcount, ratings and pay position are all in place, so building in this order kept the data model honest.

The four workflows

WF1 · Built first

Headcount reporting

Monthly headcount upload with format checks, KPI calculation and a log of every pipeline run. Produces active headcount, attrition rate, compa-ratio by grade and headcount against budget.

WF4 · Built second

Performance management

Weekly one-on-ones feed monthly check-ins, which roll up into a quarterly review and a formal rating. Ratings feed merit eligibility and attrition scoring.

WF2 · Built third

Compensation review

A six-gate eligibility engine, budget pool management, manager recommendation forms and an HR approval step, with budget use tracked live as recommendations come in.

WF3 · Built fourth

Attrition risk

A seven-factor scoring engine gives each employee a red, amber or green status, a place in a ranked risk register and a log of retention actions.

Merit eligibility engine

Six gates, checked in order. An employee must pass all six to be eligible for the merit cycle. Each gate shows its own result, so a manager can see exactly why someone is not eligible and when they next will be.

GateRule
1. Employment statusWF2-FR-004Active at the date the cycle opens
2. Minimum tenureWF2-FR-005Has completed the set minimum months of employment
3. No active PIPWF2-FR-006No active performance improvement plan on record
4. Rating thresholdWF2-FR-007Most recent formal rating meets or beats the set minimum
5. Merit recencyWF2-FR-008The minimum time since the last merit increase has passed
6. Budget confirmedWF2-FR-009Headcount budget for the role is confirmed for the cycle

Attrition risk scoring engine

Seven factors, each scored on its own, weighted and added into one risk score from 0 to 100 that maps to red, amber or green. HR admins can change the weights without a code change.

FactorWhat raises the score
Compa-ratio positionWF3-FR-002Pay low against the grade midpoint
Rating trendWF3-FR-003Ratings declining over a rolling window
Time since meritWF3-FR-004More months since the last merit increase
Time in roleWF3-FR-005A long time in the same position, read together with the other factors
Sentiment signalWF3-FR-006Concern flags and a negative pattern in weekly one-on-one notes
Check-in frequencyWF3-FR-007Missed monthly check-ins
Role market riskWF3-FR-008A manual flag for high-demand roles with strong outside pull

Scores are a prompt for a conversation between a manager and HR, not a decision about a person. Weights are visible and adjustable, and every change is logged.

Why rules instead of a prediction model

A rules engine can be audited, explained to a manager in one sentence and changed without an engineer. A prediction model needs a long, clean history of resignations that most mid-size employers do not have in structured form. Prediction stays a later phase, once at least 12 months of structured data exists.

Pairs with Comp Dash

Comp Dash works at the level of roles: which job families sit below the market. Cadence works at the level of people: who in those roles scores highest on attrition risk. Together they point at the question HR leaders actually ask: who are we most likely to lose, and is pay part of the reason?

What this shows

  • Running HR processes as governed workflows, with structured intake, clear handoffs and an audit trail for every change.
  • Merit-cycle operations (eligibility, budget tracking and approvals) written as rules anyone can inspect, instead of buried in a spreadsheet.
  • A data model that keeps headcount, performance and pay consistent: 13 PostgreSQL tables, with the links between workflows enforced in the schema.
  • Process maps written before code: ten years in HR and operations, turned into written process requirements.
  • An HR tool designed with an analyst’s discipline: every rule written down before it was built, across 45 functional requirements.

Limits

Cadence is a personal project built on synthetic employee data. It is not a production system and has not been used at an employer. The merit and attrition rules are illustrative: in a real organization they would be set with Total Rewards and legal review.
