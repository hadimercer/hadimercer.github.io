Compensation Analytics

Compensation & Market
Benchmarking Dashboard

An end-to-end data pipeline and analytics dashboard that pulls live BLS wage data, maps it to internal job families, and surfaces pay equity gaps and market positioning for HR and compensation teams.

StatusLive
Tech StackPython · PostgreSQL · Streamlit · BLS API
BA ArtifactsFRD · UML Sequence · Data Dictionary · SOC Crosswalk

The Problem

Compensation teams at mid-market companies rely on manual exports from commercial survey vendors and static spreadsheets to benchmark roles. These snapshots age quickly, don't connect to internal salary data automatically, and give no systematic view of where pay gaps or equity issues exist.

This project simulates TechNova, a fictional 500-person US tech company operating across five metros, and builds the analytics infrastructure that answers: are we paying competitively, and where are our equity risks?

What It Does

  • Pulls live BLS OEWS wage data via API — P25, P50, P75 percentiles for all roles across 5 US MSAs (Austin, New York, San Francisco, Washington DC, Denver)
  • Loads internal salary data via validated CSV upload, mapping roles through a custom SOC crosswalk
  • Displays internal vs. market side-by-side with percentile toggle and below-market flags
  • Surfaces pay equity gap flags based on statistical analysis of salary distribution within job families
  • Logs all pipeline runs with error detection and data freshness indicator

What This Demonstrates

  • End-to-end requirements: BABOK-aligned FRD covering 18 functional requirements with MoSCoW prioritisation and traceability
  • API integration: OpenAPI/Swagger spec documented for the BLS OEWS data feed
  • Data modelling: PostgreSQL schema with validated ingestion pipeline and schema-level checks before any write operation
  • UML: Sequence diagram documenting the full data flow from BLS API to dashboard render
  • BA scoping discipline: explicit in-scope and out-of-scope decisions documented (no HRIS integration, no offer workflow, US-only)

Standalone Project · Process Analytics · IT Service Management

IncidentOps —
Process Mining Insights Hub

A process mining analytics platform built on a real-world-shaped IT service desk event log. Surfaces how incidents actually flow, where time is lost, and which patterns drive poor customer outcomes.

StatusLive
Tech StackPython · PostgreSQL · Streamlit · Process Mining
BA ArtifactsFRD · BPMN · Stakeholder Map · Constraints Log

The Problem

A fictional mid-market enterprise IT service desk captures detailed event logs — ticket creation, assignments, escalations, resolution, closure, and customer feedback. But leadership relies on manual exports and surface-level averages, with no consistent view of how incidents actually flow end-to-end.

The primary failure mode is decision-making without process visibility. The same incident type can follow materially different paths (variants) with different cycle times, escalation patterns, and customer satisfaction outcomes. Without a process mining lens, teams cannot distinguish healthy flow from costly patterns like escalation ping-pong or premature closure.

What It Does

  • Discovers and visualises the top process variants — how incidents actually travel through the service desk vs. the intended path
  • Quantifies step-level dwell time and waiting time to pinpoint true bottlenecks vs. active work time
  • Measures escalation and handoff churn, detecting ping-pong patterns that consume L2/L3 specialist capacity
  • Monitors closure governance — captures premature closures leading to reopens and correlates with customer satisfaction
  • Generates a repeatable KPI pack with one-click export for weekly ops review and problem management intake

What This Demonstrates

  • Process mining thinking: going beyond averages to variant analysis and bottleneck quantification
  • BABOK-aligned FRD with 5 structured objectives, stakeholder archetypes, and full scope definition
  • BPMN: observed flow mapped against recommended process controls — both as-is and to-be perspectives
  • BA scoping rigour: analytics-only scope explicitly bounded (no ticket actions, no live ITSM integration) with rationale documented
  • Operational thinking: outputs designed for three distinct stakeholder audiences — Process Owner, Operations Manager, and Quality/CX Lead

Portfolio Health · SaaS Application

Meridian — Workstream
Portfolio Health Dashboard

A full-stack multi-user SaaS application where professionals track concurrent workstreams and receive calculated RAG health status — derived automatically from milestone data, budget burn, and blocker age. No manual colour-picking.

StatusLive
Tech StackPython · Supabase Auth · PostgreSQL RLS · Streamlit
BA Artifacts35-FR FRD · UML · BPMN · Data Dictionary

The Problem

Project managers have Jira and Asana. But business analysts, consultants, and professionals who manage work as a by-product of doing their job don't think of themselves as project managers — those tools are abandoned within weeks.

The fallback is a spreadsheet with a RAG column updated when there's time on Friday afternoon. That spreadsheet doesn't alert when a workstream is drifting. It shows whatever was last typed.

Meridian's central design principle: health status is calculated, not declared. A 9-question context wizard tunes the scoring engine per workstream — a hard-deadline client engagement is scored differently from a self-imposed internal initiative.

What It Does

  • Calculates composite RAG from three weighted dimensions: Schedule Health (milestone pace vs. elapsed time), Budget Health (planned vs. actual burn), and Blocker Health (unresolved blocker age)
  • 9-question context wizard at setup tunes scoring thresholds — 15 modifier rules documented in the FRD
  • Multi-user collaboration with role-based access (Owner / Contributor / Viewer), invite link system, and per-entity comment threads
  • Staleness flag fires when update cadence exceeds the configured threshold
  • Portfolio analytics: health trend over time, scatter matrix across workstreams, milestone velocity

What This Demonstrates

  • Full product thinking: problem statement, stakeholder personas, scoping decisions, and design rationale all documented before a line of code was written
  • 35-requirement BABOK-aligned FRD with MoSCoW prioritisation and traceability matrix
  • Database design: PostgreSQL schema with 15+ tables, FK constraints, and row-level security via Supabase
  • Auth and security: Supabase Auth, RLS on all user-scoped tables, no credentials in source code
  • UML class diagram documenting the full data model; BPMN covering workstream lifecycle and invite sub-process

HR Workflow Automation

Cadence — HR Process
Automation Hub

A structured workflow automation tool for common HR processes — onboarding, offboarding, leave management, and policy acknowledgements — replacing ad-hoc email chains with auditable, traceable task flows.

StatusLive
Tech StackPython · PostgreSQL · Streamlit · Supabase
BA ArtifactsFRD · BPMN · UML · Data Dictionary

The Problem

HR teams in mid-market organizations run their core processes — onboarding, offboarding, leave approvals — through email chains, shared spreadsheets, and institutional memory. There's no audit trail, no consistent handoff, and no visibility into where a process currently stands.

When processes are invisible, they're unimprovable. Cadence creates structured intake, automated task routing, and a full audit trail for the processes HR runs most frequently.

What It Does

  • Structured intake forms for onboarding, offboarding, leave requests, and policy acknowledgements — each mapped to a defined process flow
  • Automated task generation and routing based on process type and role configuration
  • Full audit trail — every status change, approval, and action timestamped and attributed
  • Process visibility dashboard showing in-flight cases, overdue tasks, and completion rates by process type
  • BPMN-documented process flows for each HR workflow — as-is and to-be modelled before build

What This Demonstrates

  • Process improvement discipline: as-is process documented in BPMN before any solution was designed
  • Domain expertise applied analytically: 10 years of HR operations translated into formal process requirements
  • Requirements engineering: FRD with stakeholder objectives, MoSCoW prioritisation, and NFR coverage
  • Data modelling: schema designed to support audit trail integrity and process state management
  • BA framing of an operational domain — not an HR tool built by an HR professional, but a process solution designed by an analyst who happens to know the domain
